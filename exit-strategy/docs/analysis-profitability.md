# Profitability Analysis — the Garry Tan Lens

Written 2026-07-20. Assumption-flagged numbers are replaced with real
quotes/data as they arrive; gate reviews land in `decisions.md`.

The Tan test is not "is the idea good." It is: do people want it, can you
reach them repeatedly without buying every customer, are you default alive,
and does the founder have an unfair advantage in this exact market. Applied
honestly below — including the parts that argue against us.

## 1. What is genuinely strong here

1. **Build cost is approximately zero.** The payments and attribution spine
   — normally a contractor quarter — was built in one AI session for $0
   marginal cost. Engineering, content pipelines, print files, tuning
   simulations: all AI-side labor. The only meaningful cash outlay in the
   entire plan is the print run. Companies die of burn; this one has almost
   none to die of.
2. **Default alive by construction.** Fixed costs are hosting and domains
   (≈$25–50/mo). There is no scenario where standing still costs real
   money. The only way to lose a meaningful sum is to buy inventory nobody
   wants — which is exactly what the gates below exist to prevent.
3. **The series is the business, not the deck.** One $24.99 deck is a
   product; five Plays with shared codes, one community, and repeat
   purchase is a customer relationship with LTV ≈ 3–5× first order. The
   expanding-series model is what makes small-margin physical goods
   compound.
4. **A real distribution wedge.** The in-person share loop is native to the
   product fiction (Rolodex literally is the mechanic), the founder brings
   the community/author platform and the on-camera presence, and Buy,
   Borrow, Die is a legitimately screenshottable hook. Most board games
   have zero distribution story; this one has three.
5. **The ethical guardrails are a moat, not a tax.** "The share program
   that structurally cannot be a pyramid" is a differentiated, defensible
   story in a category where the incumbent (Cashflow) is sold through
   seminar upsells. We can prove ours in CI. Nobody else can say that.

## 2. What kills companies exactly like this (said plainly)

1. **Inventory bought ahead of demand.** The single most common death in
   physical games. A garage of 900 unsold decks is the failure mode; every
   sequencing decision below exists to make it impossible.
2. **K > 1 for a physical product is nearly unheard of.** The spec targets
   it; treat it as upside, never as the plan. The plan must be profitable
   at K ≈ 0.3–0.5 (shares reduce CAC) or it is not a plan.
3. **The category is crowded and low-signal.** "Financial literacy game"
   on Amazon is a graveyard of well-meaning decks. The brand question and
   the spicy plays are the differentiation; if content doesn't convert,
   the category won't save us. Amazon is a later flywheel, not a launch
   strategy (consistent with spec §4.3's DTC-first).
4. **Revenue-share overhead.** Real accounting, age gating, payout rails,
   and state-level compliance questions. Manageable — but it must stay
   smaller than the growth it buys, and payouts stay accrual-only until a
   compliance decision (already flagged in the Phase 0 ticket).
5. **A two-person company where one person is the face.** Content
   cadence, playtests, and community are founder-bound. The plan below
   minimizes founder hours everywhere else so those hours exist.

## 4. The gates — how confidence is earned (proposed D-006)

I am not asserting demand; nobody honestly can. The structure lets us buy
the answer for ≈$0 before the print check is written. Confidence is
staged:

- **G1 — demand signal ($0 at risk).** Free online Escape Velocity live.
  Pass: ≥40% of new players complete a first game, ≥15% start a second
  within 7 days, over ≥200 players. Fail → tune or rethink the flagship;
  no dollars were at risk.
- **G2 — wallet signal (caps the print risk).** Public preorder at $24.99
  via Stripe. Pass: **≥150 paid preorders** (15% of the run, ≈60% of
  breakeven) before the print vendor is paid. Fail → iterate content or
  kill; refunds cost clicks, not inventory.
- **G3 — loop signal (gates scale-up).** Before run 2 or any paid CAC:
  blended K ≥ 0.35, in-person ≥ 30% of weighted shares, ≥ 25% of shipped
  decks redeeming their code. Pass → the series economics compound and
  game 2 ships. Fail → fix the loop before spending on reach.

Each gate review is a decision-log entry with the real numbers, pass or
fail.

## 5. Verdict

**Conditionally confident — and the conditions are cheap.** With
contribution margin of ~$14–18/deck, fixed burn near zero, build labor
near zero, and total downside capped at ≈$5k *only after* ≥150 strangers
have already paid, this is profitable at small scale if G1 and G2 pass —
and G1/G2 cost approximately nothing to run. The expertise split matches
the risk: the founder's leverage (face, community, in-person world) is
exactly the part AI cannot do, and everything else in the plan is mine to
execute. What I will not do is promise demand before measuring it; the
gates exist so that by the time real money moves, confidence is a reading,
not a feeling.
